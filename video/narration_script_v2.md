# Narration script v2 (EN): one line per scene, in recording order

record_v7.cjs holds each scene for its line's audio length (narration_v2/nNN.wav) when the file exists. Scene notes in parentheses are not read.

Lines 8, 9, 12 and 13 were rewritten on 2026-11-15 after the independent review (baseline run before the rules are shown; 16 of 44 separable pieces; newcomers). Their audio is n08b, n09b, n12b and n13b.wav; the first takes (n08, n09, n12, n13.wav) and the unused n04b.wav are kept but not used.

1. (whole cup, the question on screen) You're holding an empty coffee cup. Paper, plastic, or trash? The honest answer: it depends on where you're standing, and on a layer you can't see.
2. (cross-section) Teardown cuts it open. Inside the paper is a thin plastic film, bonded on. You can't peel it off at home.
3. (take apart, parts fly to the bins) Take it apart, and let four cities sort it by their own official rules.
4. (four cities sorted) Seoul takes the cup as paper. Berlin, as packaging. San Francisco, as recycling. Shinjuku, in Tokyo, sends it to burnable waste. Four cities, four different answers.
5. (click the cup: source panel) Every move is backed by the city's own sentence, in its own language, with a link to the source.
6. (your city: San Francisco) So what do you do with it? Pick your city, and the rules become moves. In San Francisco, two. Cup, sleeve and lid in the blue cart. The stirrer in compost.
7. (learned in Tokyo) Learned to sort somewhere else? Teardown shows only the habits that change. The cup you burned in Tokyo is recycled here.
8. (guess first: a pizza box, a wrong answer, then the rule) Or guess first. Take a pizza box, before the page has shown you any rule. One part at a time, every bin the city has. Only the first try counts, and a wrong answer is corrected with the city's own sentence.
9. (baseline, the city's moves, run 2) That run is the baseline. Read the city's moves, run it again, and the log holds both: first-try accuracy, before and after. That is how we would measure whether it works.
10. (bottle: Seoul and Berlin) A water bottle. In Seoul, the label comes off. In Berlin, it stays on, and the whole bottle goes back to the shop for the deposit. One move.
11. (phone, four cities) A phone is the one thing all four cities agree on. Keep it whole, and hand it in.
12. (what we found) Add it up, and the pattern is clear. Sorting fails at the seams. Three of four objects are sorted differently from city to city. Of the forty-four pieces you can separate by hand, sixteen have no rule in the official guide. And objects that go back whole work everywhere.
13. (so what) So residents get the moves and the practice. Newcomers get only the habits that change. Cities get the list of parts their guide never names. And designers get one rule: fewer seams.
14. (how it is built) It is a static page built with three dot j s. Every model is generated in code, and every rule sits in one file, with its source.
15. (end) Teardown. See what it's made of. Sort it where you are.
